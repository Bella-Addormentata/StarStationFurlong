# IPv6 knock between two home computers, 2026-11-18

> **What this is:** the record of the run that proved the IPv6 note ([#467](https://github.com/Bella-Addormentata/StarStationFurlong/pull/467)) between the owner's two computers, on two providers. The holder's firewall blocks what comes in for the node, and the IPv4 knock was switched off on both sides.
>
> **Result:** with the knock off, no link in 6 minutes: all 8 dial attempts timed out. With only the IPv6 note on, the room linked on the first dial attempt, 30 s after the guest's page started. The holder logged the connection coming in from the guest's IPv6 address 4 s after its opening began.
>
> **How to read it:** times are UTC. "Holder" is the laptop holding the room, "guest" the desktop joining it. Computer names, IP addresses, invites and room keys are left out; node ids are cut short. Where a statement is an inference and not a log line, it says so.

## 2. What it shows

- **The note carries what the IPv4 knock cannot.** With the IPv4 knock off on both sides, the holder learned the guest's IPv6 addresses only from the note. It opened its firewall toward them, and the guest's running dial came in over IPv6.
- **Without the note, the holder's firewall kept the guest out** (the control run). So the real run's link was not the guest's dial getting in by itself.
- **It is quick.** From the guest's dial to the link took 29 s. The note's read, merge, write and the check 10 s later took 18 s of that, and the holder, which reads its notes every 30 s, opened 2 s after the guest logged its note.
- **The holder opened once more, later, and got nothing** (inferred: its log has no timestamps). There are three "knocked … (its IPv6 note)" lines and two "nothing came in … in 180 s" lines. One opening per guest address came at the knock, and one of them got the connection. The third most likely came after the guest had stopped, while its note entry still stood (10 minutes). The same as TODO's knock follow-up (8) describes for the IPv4 knock, and bounded by the same caps.

## 3. Setup

- **Holder:** a Windows laptop on Wi-Fi with one provider: public IPv4 behind its router, two global IPv6 addresses, Windows firewall on (Public profile) with inbound blocked for the node. The node used the default ports (UDP 44442, WebTransport 4443, API 8080).
- **Guest:** a Windows desktop with another provider and two global IPv6 addresses. The installed game kept running beside the test on its own ports. The test node used UDP 44452, WebTransport 4453 and API 8081, with `SSF_EXTERNAL_ADDRS` removed for that process only.
- **Both:** a release build with `chia-publish`, `SSF_NO_CHIA_LANE=1`, `SSF_NO_MDNS=1`, no relay, the real Mainline DHT. On each side: the node, the page's dev server on loopback, and `scripts/drill-link.mjs` driving a headless Edge as the one player.

Not run: both knocks on (the default), and the case the IPv4 knock leaves open (a holder behind a router that gives every destination its own port, reached over IPv6).
